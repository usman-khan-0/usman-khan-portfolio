The Company

Overview · HMC

Established in 1971 with Chinese cooperation — a strategic hub for heavy mechanical engineering

Mother Engineering Industry

HMC is Pakistan's "Mother Engineering Industry" — a strategic hub for heavy mechanical engineering. Over six weeks, I rotated through production planning, machine shops, fabrication, forging, pattern shop, and foundries.

What They Build

Heavy machinery, mill cheeks, rollers, pinions, sprockets, fans, and large fabricated structures. Everything from raw plate and billet to assembled, finished mechanical systems.

Engineering in Practice

What I Learned · Machines & Processes

From PPC to the foundry floor — every machine and process told a story of precision, force, and industrial discipline.

Product Planning & Control

PPC orchestrates production: scheduling, resource allocation, quality checks. I learned HMC's 6-digit sales order system (XXYYZZ) and 8-digit inventory numbering — making material tracking seamless. It was my first look at how an industrial operation stays organised.

Lathe Machine

The universal machine for cylindrical precision. I observed turning, facing, drilling, boring, threading, knurling, and parting operations. The lathe removes material from a rotating workpiece using a stationary cutting tool.

Medium & Large bays scale up the same machines for bigger workpieces. Assembly bay: we saw mill cheeks, rollers, pinions, sprockets, and fans being assembled.

Tools & Measurement

Precision is non-negotiable. I used vernier calipers, height gauges, depth gauges, try squares, and steel rules. I also observed tool sharpening — grinding worn cutting tools to restore geometry and extend life. In the marking section, they laid out ellipses and hole positions on drum heads using dividers and red paint.

Fabrication Shop

Welding: SAW (Submerged Arc) for heavy straight welds. TIG & MIG for precision and medium sections. Cutting: Plasma, CNC gas, and auto-semi plasma cutting for plate profiles. Hot forming: Press machines and rolling sections shape plates into cylinders and structural forms. Learn more about welding processes

Forging Shop · Steam Hammers & Hydraulic Press

Machine info

Steam Hammer

Steam pressure raises a heavy ram which falls onto the heated workpiece. The repeated impact forces gradually deform the hot metal. HMC has four steam hammers: one 1-ton, two 2-ton, and one 3-ton.

Impact Forging 1-3 Ton Capacity
Learn more about steam hammers
Machine info

Hydraulic Press

The 3007 hydraulic press applies controlled, continuous force for specialized forming operations. Unlike a hammer's impact, hydraulic pressing offers gradual, accurately controlled force — essential for precise heavy forming.

Controlled Forming 3000-ton Class
Learn more about hydraulic presses

Steam hammers deliver impact forging. The 3007 hydraulic press applies controlled continuous force. I observed the whole sequence: heating → pre-forming → die forging → trimming (1000-ton trim press). The boiler house supplies steam at 6 bar / 250°C to run the hammers.

Pattern Shop · Cast Iron & Steel Foundry

Machine info

Induction Furnace

Electromagnetic induction generates heat to melt metal. The alternating current in the coil induces eddy currents in the metal charge, creating resistive heating. HMC uses induction furnaces for controlled, efficient melting.

Electromagnetic Melting 1150-1200°C
Learn more about induction furnaces

Patterns include allowances: machining, shrinkage, taper (draft). Prints, risers, core bursts, cope & drag — every element ensures a defect-free casting. In the Cast Iron Foundry: silica sand, facing sand, induction furnaces (1150-1200°C melting) and cupola furnaces. Steel Foundry: Electric Arc Furnace (15-ton) with PLC control and argon purging, plus induction furnace (1000 kg). Green sand for light castings, silica sand for heavy.
